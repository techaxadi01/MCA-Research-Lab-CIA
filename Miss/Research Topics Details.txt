### 1. Research Area

**Marine Ecological Informatics & Bio-Optical Remote Sensing** (Spatio-Temporal Data Science applied to Oceanographic Systems / SDG 14: Life Below Water)

---

### 2. Methodology & MCA Technical Concepts Used

The methodology integrates **Bio-Optical Remote Sensing Inversion** with **Multimodal Oceanographic Time-Series Analytics**:

* **Satellite Digital Image Processing (Spatial Grid Transformation):** Extraction and calibration of Level-3 multispectral grids ($R_{rs}$—Remote Sensing Reflectance at discrete wavelengths: $443\text{ nm}$, $488\text{ nm}$, $531\text{ nm}$, $555\text{ nm}$, and $678\text{ nm}$) from MODIS-Aqua and Sentinel-3 OLCI.
* **Multidimensional Data Handling:** Parsing high-dimensional raster formats (NetCDF-4 / HDF5 / GeoTIFF) using spatial array manipulation libraries (`numpy`, `xarray`) to extract latitude, longitude, and depth-stratified temporal slices.
* **Spectral Feature Engineering & Ratio Inversion:** Formulating normalized difference indices and band reflectance ratios (e.g., $R_{rs}(443)/R_{rs}(555)$ and fluorescence line height) to discriminate green endosymbiont pigmentation from generic chlorophyll signatures.
* **Exploratory Data Science & Anomaly Detection:** Data cleaning, spatial outlier filtering, temporal interpolation across cloud-masked gaps, and cross-correlation heatmaps relating ocean color spikes to abiotic triggers (Sea Surface Temperature and Dissolved Oxygen deficits).

---

### 3. Problem Statement

Intense winter and post-monsoon blooms of the mixotrophic dinoflagellate *Noctiluca scintillans* cause extensive green discoloration across the Northern and Eastern Arabian Sea, triggering coastal hypoxia, marine mortality, and disruption to local fisheries. Standard ocean color satellite models (such as standard NASA OCx/Chlorophyll-$a$ algorithms) fail in these waters because they rely on broad blue-green reflectance ratios that frequently misclassify non-toxic diatom blooms, suspended sediments, and sea surface foam as harmful algal blooms (HABs). Furthermore, existing monitoring frameworks lack a reproducible data pipeline that directly couples multispectral optical indices with localized physical triggers (such as sea surface temperature anomalies and mixed-layer shoaling) to track the bloom from its initial proliferation through its oxygen-depleting decay phase.

---

### 4. Research Objectives

1. **Formulate a Bio-Optical Spectral Index Pipeline:** Systematically process Level-3 multispectral satellite rasters to isolate the characteristic reflectance signatures ($443\text{ nm}$–$555\text{ nm}$) of green *Noctiluca scintillans* and differentiate them from native diatom blooms and coastal turbidity noise.
2. **Standardize a Multimodal Preprocessing & Cleansing Protocol:** Develop a pipeline to ingest and normalize heterogenous oceanographic NetCDF datasets (MODIS/Sentinel-3 ocean color alongside Copernicus CMEMS physical reanalysis), handling missing pixels due to seasonal cloud masking through robust spatio-temporal interpolation.
3. **Analyze Multi-Parametric Environmental Triggers:** Conduct statistical Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) to map correlation patterns and time-lag relationships between sea surface temperature (SST) drops, dissolved oxygen (DO) depletion, and peak algal bloom intensity.
4. **Characterize Spatio-Temporal Bloom Dynamics:** Profile the life-cycle progression (onset, peak discoloration, and subsequent hypoxia-inducing decay phase) to provide an empirical basis for coastal early-warning reporting in an IEEE-standard conference manuscript.

---

### 5. Research Gap Identified

* **Spectral Ambiguity in Standard Blue-Green Inversions:** Current operational satellite monitoring over-relies on standard empirical Chlorophyll-$a$ algorithms ($OC3$/$OC4$), which cannot distinguish the green mixotrophic endosymbionts of *Noctiluca* from harmless siliceous diatoms or suspended terrigenous matter in optically complex coastal waters (Case-2 waters).
* **Decoupling of Optical Signals from Biogeochemical Drivers:** Most remote sensing studies document blooms solely after green discoloration appears at the surface. There is a lack of an integrated computational data framework that correlates pre-bloom sea surface cooling and vertical mixing with post-bloom subsurface dissolved oxygen collapse.
* **Absence of a Reproducible Open-Data Benchmark:** Existing investigations are largely split between purely biological shipboard sampling (spatially constrained) and proprietary physical models. A standardized, reproducible data pipeline linking open-access satellite repositories (NASA OceanColor, ESA Copernicus) to exploratory analytics remains unavailable for regional coastal managers.
